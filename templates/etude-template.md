## Etude Design Guidelines

When generating etudes for a session, follow this progression:

1. **Etude 1 — Foundations**
   - Isolated concept, minimal chord changes
   - Slow tempo, focus on clean execution
   - Example: Just the core shape or progression, whole notes

2. **Etude 2 — Add Rhythm/Movement**
   - Same harmonic content, add rhythmic interest
   - Introduce voice leading or melodic movement
   - Example: Add a strumming pattern or arpeggiation

3. **Etude 3 — Musical Context**
   - Apply the concept in a short musical phrase
   - Could be a verse section, a turnaround, or a loop
   - Example: 8-bar section that feels like a real song

4. **Etude 4 — Challenge**
   - Combine with a related concept or increase complexity
   - Higher tempo, more demanding transitions
   - Example: Add extensions, modulations, or a solo break

Keep each etude **short and focused** — 4-16 bars max. The goal is repetition
and muscle memory, not marathon sight-reading.
