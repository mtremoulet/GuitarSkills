Crunchy, middy, harmonic distortion, singing sustain. Cream, Led Zeppelin, Rolling Stones, AC/DC.

Three Rules That Override Everything
1Roll guitar volume to 7 for all jazz and clean blues tones — this activates the Tone King's touch-sensitivity and dynamic range. Full guitar volume is for classic rock and Texas blues only.
2Mid-Bite is cumulative with pickup character. P-90s (Framus) already push upper mids naturally — they need 0.5–1.0 less Mid-Bite than the LP to reach the same tonal target.
3Sheraton feedback risk at high gain. With Mid-Bite above 5.0, the semi-hollow body wants to feed back. Keep Attenuation at 5.5 max, position away from monitors. The payoff is singing sustain solid-bodies can't produce.
Gibson Les Paul Studio — Pickup Specs
2014 Gibson Les Paul Studio, 120th Anniversary, matte black. Fitted with 490R neck and 490T bridge — both Alnico II, ~8.5k DCR. This is a matched pair: both pickups share the same magnet type and similar output level, producing a balanced, warm, vintage character across all pickup positions. The 490T bridge is notably lower-output and warmer than the 498T commonly found in LP Standards or Traditionals (~14.5k DCR, Alnico V). Classic Rock and Texas Blues bridge settings in this guide are calibrated for the 490T — they require slightly more Volume and Mid-Bite than a hotter 498T would need.
Framus Earl Slick — Pickup Character
The Framus Earl Slick Artist Series has DiMarzio P-90s — single-coil wound pickups in soapbar housings, not traditional humbuckers. P-90s are brighter, spankier, and more mid-forward than humbuckers. They need less Treble and less Mid-Bite than the LP to achieve equivalent tonal targets. The natural P-90 character is an asset in neo-soul, blues, and classic rock — work with it rather than against it.
