**Created:** 2026-05-28

## Hardware Controls Reference

**Tone King Imperial Preamp controls by channel:**

| Channel | Controls |
|---------|----------|
| Rhythm | Volume, Attenuation, Bass, Treble |
| Lead | Volume, Attenuation, Tone (high-freq contour), Mid-Bite (gain + upper-mid boost + bass tighten — simultaneously) |

There is no Mid, Presence, or Cut knob on either channel.

**Gibson Les Paul Studio (2014, 120th Anniversary, matte black):** 490R neck and **490T** bridge — both Alnico II, ~8.5k DCR. A matched pair, warm and balanced. The 490T bridge is lower-output and warmer than the 498T often found in LP Standards; Classic Rock and Texas Blues bridge settings are calibrated for this.

**Framus Earl Slick Artist Series:** DiMarzio **P-90s** — single-coil wound pickups in soapbar housings, not traditional humbuckers. Brighter, spankier, and more mid-forward than humbuckers; need less Mid-Bite and treble than the LP to reach the same tonal target.

**Scale:** All values use a 0–10 scale. 5.0 = noon/flat. Attenuation 5.0 = unity/moderate output.

## The Three Rules That Override Everything

1. **Roll guitar volume to 7** for all jazz and clean blues tones — this activates the Tone King's touch-sensitivity. Full guitar volume is for classic rock and Texas blues only.
2. **Mid-Bite is cumulative** — P-90s already push upper mids naturally, so the Framus needs 0.5–1.0 less Mid-Bite than the LP to reach the same character.
3. **Semi-hollow feedback risk** — On the Sheraton with Mid-Bite above 5.0, the body wants to feed back. Keep Attenuation at 5.5 max and position away from monitors.

# JAZZY

*Target: Clean, dark, warm, full low-mids, zero grit. Wes Montgomery, Ed Bickert, Jim Hall.*

The **Rhythm channel** is primary for jazz. The Lead channel can work with Mid-Bite ≤ 2.0 — it adds the faintest upper-mid warmth without crunch. IR: **OH 112 Imperial TK1660** on all guitars.

# YACHT ROCK

*Target: Silky clean, polished, bright-warm, glassy transparency. Christopher Cross, Michael McDonald, late-70s/early-80s AOR.*

The **Rhythm channel is the only natural fit here** — blackface clean at maximum headroom. The Lead channel's tweed character doesn't belong in this style. IR: **OH 212 Class A Blue** (Vox 2x12 chime matches AOR polish).

---

### Gibson LP

| Channel | Pickup | Guitar Vol / Tone | Volume | Attenuation | Bass | Treble | Reverb |
|---------|--------|-------------------|--------|-------------|------|--------|--------|
| **Rhythm** | Bridge (490T) | 9 / 8 | 2.5 | 5.0 | 5.0 | 7.0 | On: Level 1.5, Dwell 2.0 |

# CLASSIC ROCK

*Target: Crunchy, middy, harmonic distortion, singing sustain. Cream, Led Zeppelin, Rolling Stones, AC/DC.*

The **Lead channel with Mid-Bite at 5.5–7.0** is the engine here. Mid-Bite transforms 50s tweed into British crunch by simultaneously raising gain, tightening bass, and pushing upper mids. The Rhythm channel at high Volume gives a "pushed Fender" character (more SRV/early Stones). IR: **OH 412 Basketweave M25**.

---

> **Rhythm/Fender crunch:** Volume 7.0 compensates for the 490T's moderate output (Bass 4.5, Treble 7.0). Think early Stones or SRV's clean end.
