# Ambient Bath Scoop

For ambient guitar playing, sound-baths, and long delay/reverb trails, the biggest challenge is clutter. When you feed a mid-forward guitar signal into a 10-second reverb decay, the midrange quickly builds up into an unpleasant, muddy cloud of sound.

This EQprint solves this by scooping the core midrange (around 1kHz) where notes build up. It preserves low-mid warmth (250Hz) and boosts high-end air (8kHz to 16kHz) to create an ethereal, spacious texture where the wet reverb trail has room to breathe.

## Why this curve works (Band-by-Band)

- **31.25 Hz & 62.5 Hz (Cut to -12dB and -8dB)**: Crucial low-end cuts. In ambient playing, sub-bass reverb tails can quickly swamp the entire mix and rumble the speakers.
- **125 Hz & 250 Hz (Cut to -2dB and Boost to +2dB)**: We keep a slight scoop at 125Hz but bump 250Hz slightly. This provides warmth and structural support to the notes without creating bass mud.
- **500 Hz, 1 kHz & 2 kHz (Cut to -2dB, -5dB, and -2dB)**: This is the ambient scoop. By dipping the 1kHz range, we clear out the "clutter zone." This lets the reverb reflections float around the notes rather than colliding with them.
- **4 kHz (Boost to +2dB)**: Restores string articulation so you can still hear the initial note definition when strumming.
- **8 kHz & 16 kHz (Boost to +4dB and +6dB)**: Boosts the high-frequency air. This highlights the "shimmer" and "halo" effect on reverb trails, giving the ambient bath a glassy, ethereal finish.
