# Mid-Hump Lead Boost

When a guitar needs to transition from rhythm to a lead solo, simply increasing the volume isn't enough—it often just overloads the low-end and creates muddiness. The secret to a solo cutting through a full band mix is midrange focus.

This EQprint replicates the classic Tube Screamer curve. It filters out flabby sub-bass frequencies (which eat up amp headroom under gain) and boosts the 1kHz vocal frequency range, while slightly smoothing out the high-end to prevent drive fizz.

## Why this curve works (Band-by-Band)

- **31.25 Hz, 62.5 Hz & 125 Hz (Cut to -12dB, -8dB, and -4dB)**: Bass cuts are essential before a gain stage (pre-amp). High levels of low frequency under gain lead to a loose, flabby distortion known as "bass blocking distortion." Cutting this keeps the drive tight and punchy.
- **250 Hz (-1dB)**: Kept slightly below neutral to prevent muddy build-up.
- **500 Hz & 1 kHz (Boost to +4dB and +6dB)**: The "mid-hump" core. 1kHz is where the human ear is highly sensitive and where the guitar's fundamental lead notes live. Pushing this pushes the guitar forward in the sonic landscape.
- **2 kHz (Boost to +3dB)**: Adds bite and string projection.
- **4 kHz (0dB)**: Left flat to preserve natural amp presence.
- **8 kHz & 16 kHz (Cut to -4dB and -8dB)**: Rolls off ultra-high harmonics. This prevents the distortion from sounding like "bees in a can" or getting ice-picky.
- **Level (Boost to +2dB)**: Provides a slight volume push to drive the preamp or overdrive pedal into compression.
