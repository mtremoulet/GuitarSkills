# Velvet Sheraton Humbucker

Humbuckers in semi-hollow body guitars have a beautiful, thick sustain, but the neck pickup is prone to severe "mud" and "boom" in the lower registers (125Hz to 250Hz), especially when playing complex chord voicings where individual notes need to remain distinct.

This EQprint clears the mud first, then highlights the high-mid vocal range to give chord melody arrangements definition and separation, without losing the warm "velvet" wrap of the humbucker.

## Why this curve works (Band-by-Band)

- **31.25 Hz & 62.5 Hz (Cut to -12dB and -10dB)**: Standard high-pass filtering to clear out sub-bass mud and prevent cabinet vibration.
- **125 Hz & 250 Hz (Cut to -4dB and -2dB)**: This is the mud-clearing zone. Neck humbuckers have a massive resonant peak here that dominates the tone. Cutting this allows individual notes in jazz chords to ring out clearly rather than blending into a boomy wash.
- **500 Hz & 1 kHz (Boost to +2dB and +3dB)**: Boosts the vocal midrange. This highlights the fundamental frequencies of single-note lines and gives them a "singing," projecting quality that helps chord melodies stand out.
- **2 kHz (Boost to +1dB)**: Adds a touch of string articulation and pick definition so fingerstyle playing sounds clear and immediate.
- **4 kHz (Cut to -2dB)**: Slightly attenuated to ensure the high strings don't sound harsh or "plucky."
- **8 kHz & 16 kHz (Cut to -6dB and -10dB)**: Smooth roll-off of high frequencies to keep the overall character warm and dark, avoiding digital sizzle on the tail of notes.
