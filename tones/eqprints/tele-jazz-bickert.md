# Warm Jazz Bickert Veil

This EQprint is designed to create the "electronic veil" pioneered by jazz legend Ed Bickert, who famously played a Telecaster neck pickup rolled back into a warm, woody jazz-box tone. 

Telecaster neck pickups have a lovely woodiness but can suffer from low-end rumble and a high-end "click" or chime that exposes the solid-body nature of the guitar. This curve sculpts the pickup before it hits the amp to simulate the acoustic resonance of a hollow-body.

## Why this curve works (Band-by-Band)

- **31.25 Hz & 62.5 Hz (Cut to -12dB and -8dB)**: Eliminates sub-audible stage rumble and low-end cabinet flabbiness. Since the low E string fundamental is ~82Hz, everything below this is noise or unproductive energy that muddies the preamp.
- **125 Hz (Cut to -2dB)**: Gently controls the boomy boom-resonance of the low strings when playing thick thumb-picked chords, preventing the amp's input stage from overloading.
- **250 Hz & 500 Hz (Boost to +3dB and +2dB)**: Boosts the "body" and "woodiness" of the guitar. This range mimics the body chamber resonance of a hollow-body archtop.
- **1 kHz & 2 kHz (0dB and -2dB)**: Keeps the high-mids natural but slightly recessed to take away the aggressive pick attack.
- **4 kHz (Cut to -6dB)**: Smooths out the "bite" or transient click of the pick hitting the strings.
- **8 kHz & 16 kHz (Cut to -12dB)**: Full attenuation to act as a steep low-pass filter, rolling off all high-end digital "air" and fizz to simulate flatwound strings and a rolled-back tone knob.
